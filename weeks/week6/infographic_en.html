The Law of Large Numbers at Light Speed

Stochastic simulations allow us to make risks mathematically tangible. But only through modern acceleration libraries like JAX can we simulate millions of scenarios in parallel and explore complex probability distributions in real time.

Monte Carlo Integration vs. Grid Integration

As the dimension $d$ increases, the efficiency of classical integration grids completely collapses (curse of dimensionality). Monte Carlo maintains its convergence rate of $O(1/\sqrt{N})$ unimpressed.

Integration Error vs. Number of Samples (N)

Grid Integration (d=1)

Converges excellently with $O(1/N)$. In one dimension, deterministic grids are unbeatably fast.

Monte Carlo (dimension independent)

Always has the error rate $O(1/\sqrt{N})$ (equilibrium). No matter if 1 or 100 dimensions, the error curve remains identical.

Grid Integration (d=4)

The error only decreases with $O(N^{-1/4})$. To get the same error as in the one-dimensional case, you need a giant number of samples.
