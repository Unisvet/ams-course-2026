Maziar Raissi

PINN Pioneer

"Physics is not a constraint, but a guide for the neural network."

Maziar Raissi was the first to realize that we can feed neural networks not just with data, but with the fundamental conservation laws of physics. His 2017 paper shook the world of simulation. He proved that an MLP (Multilayer Perceptron) can solve the Navier-Stokes equations without ever seeing a grid.

⚡

Zongyi Li

Master of Operators

"Learn the operator, not the solution."

Zongyi Li revolutionized the field in 2020 with the Fourier Neural Operator (FNO). While other networks only learned static solutions, he developed a system that learns how functions transform. His network uses the FFT (Fast Fourier Transform) to compute in frequency space, making it 1000x faster than classical CFD solvers.

George Karniadakis

The Godfather

Professor Karniadakis of Brown University is the driving force behind SciML. He bridged the worlds of Applied Mathematics and Machine Learning. Under his leadership, PINNs, DeepONets, and countless other architectures were born. He is known for training his students to be 'SciML warriors', ready to solve the most complex problems in fluid dynamics using JAX.

PDE Magic

Universal Laws

Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) describe everything: from the flight of a bird to the heat development in your laptop. In Week 5, you'll learn how these abstract equations flow into the loss function of an AI model. It's the moment when "Black Box" deep learning becomes a "Transparent Box" physics simulation.
