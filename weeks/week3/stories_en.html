The Father of Cybernetics

Norbert Wiener recognized that everything in nature—from the human brain to a steam engine—is controlled via feedback loops. He called this field "Cybernetics" (Greek for steersman). Without his mathematical foundations of optimization and control, there would be no autonomous agents or stable simulation chains today.

"Information is information, not matter or energy."

The Universal Genius

John von Neumann was so fast in his mind that colleagues joked he was a highly developed being simulating a human. He not only invented the architecture for almost all modern computers but was also a pioneer in game theory and linear optimization. He understood computers not as mere calculators, but as tools to master the complexity of existence.

"It's pointless to discuss things that cannot be precisely defined."

The Master of Opposites

Ian Goodfellow invented GANs (Generative Adversarial Networks) after a discussion at a bar. He realized that two AIs could be pitted against each other to enforce perfection. His knowledge of the "Loss Landscape"—the mountain range of errors that an AI must navigate—is today the basis for almost all deep neural networks.

"Optimization is the battle against the local minimum."
