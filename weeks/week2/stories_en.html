The Queen of Code

Grace Hopper invented the first compiler because she believed computers should be more understandable to humans. When she found a moth in a relay of the Harvard Mark II in 1947, she taped it into her logbook and called it the "first actual case of bug being found." She proved that details are crucial.

"It's easier to ask for forgiveness than to get permission."

The Father of Learning

Arthur Samuel coined the term "Machine Learning" in 1959. He programmed a computer to play checkers against itself and learn from its mistakes. Soon, the program beat its own creator. He understood that we must not just command machines, but enable them to grow.

"Machines can do things that we cannot explain in detail."

The Architect of Giants

Jeff Dean is the driving force behind Google Brain and systems like TensorFlow. He helped design the TPUs that today perform millions of matrix multiplications per second. In the tech world, there are countless jokes about his efficiency, similar to Chuck Norris.

"Jeff Dean's code optimizes itself before it's even compiled."
